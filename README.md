# PillPal
Snap a photo of a prescription bottle to get plain-language instructions, a daily dose schedule, side effects, and interaction warnings sourced from official FDA drug labels. Built at TigerHacks 2026 (Mizzou).

PillPal turns confusing prescription labels into information anyone can understand. Take a photo of a pill bottle, confirm what the app read, and get:

Clear instructions on how and when to take your medication
A daily dose schedule with reminders, plus a visual pictogram version (icons for morning/food/etc.) for low-literacy or low-vision users
Common and serious side effects, including when to get help right away
What to avoid, such as alcohol, certain foods, or activities
Interaction warnings across all the medications you scan
Translated audio playback of the simplified instructions, for patients who read a different language than the label

Medication mistakes and missed warnings are common, especially for older adults, low-literacy or non-English-speaking patients, and people managing several prescriptions. This app helps patients and caregivers understand exactly what they're taking and how to take it safely.

Trusted sources: Side effects, warnings, and interactions come from official FDA drug labels (via openFDA), with drug names standardized using the NIH's RxNorm. AI is used only to rewrite this information in plain language, never to invent it.

Safety first: The app asks users to confirm every detail it reads, and if the label is unclear, damaged, or a reading is low-confidence, it says so and asks the user to check with a pharmacist instead of guessing. It does not provide medical advice.
